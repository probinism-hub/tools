What Is a Broken Link?

A broken link (also called a dead link) is a link that does not lead to a working page. When someone clicks it, they usually land on an error such as 404 Not Found instead of the content they expected. Links break for everyday reasons: a page is deleted or moved, a web address is changed, a website shuts down, or a link was simply typed incorrectly.

Why You Should Check for Broken Links

  • Better experience for visitors. Nobody likes clicking a link and hitting an error page. Working links keep people moving through your site.
  • Easier crawling for search engines. Search engines discover pages by following links. Dead ends can make it harder for them to find and understand your content.
  • A more trustworthy site. Outdated links make a website look neglected. Regular checks keep it looking looked-after.
  • Protect your affiliate and partner links. If a link you rely on stops working, you may be sending visitors nowhere.

Good to know: broken links are not a magic ranking switch in either direction. The point is to keep your site useful and easy to crawl. That is why fixing them is a good habit rather than a one-off job.

Understanding the Results

Working

The link opened successfully (status codes in the 200 range). No action needed.

Broken

The link failed. Typical causes are 404 Not Found or 410 Gone (the page no longer exists), 5xx errors (the other website had a server problem), or a domain that cannot be found. These are the ones to fix first.

Needs a look

The tool could not give a clear yes or no. This happens when a link redirects somewhere else, when a website refuses automated visitors (often shown as 403 or 429 even though the link works in a browser), or when a site is slow to answer. Open these links yourself to confirm.

How to Fix Broken Links

  1. Update the link if the page has moved to a new address.
  2. Redirect the old page to the most relevant new page if it was one of your own that you moved or renamed.
  3. Replace it with a similar, up-to-date resource if the original is gone for good.
  4. Remove the link if there is nothing suitable to point to.
  5. Fix typos. A surprising number of broken links are simply a mistyped address.

After making changes, run the checker again to confirm everything now works.

Tips for Keeping Your Links Healthy

  • Run a check after redesigns, migrations and big content updates.
  • Scan regularly, even when you have not changed anything. Other websites change too, and external links can break without warning.
  • When you delete or rename a page, set up a redirect straight away.
  • Prioritise broken links on your most visited pages first.

How often should I check my website for broken links?
